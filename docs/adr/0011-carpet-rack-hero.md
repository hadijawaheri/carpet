# 0011 · The hero is a carpet seller's swing rack

Date: 2026-10-23 · Status: accepted (owner asked for carpets that turn over like a seller's rack and move like real carpets)

- **The hero shows every carpet on one swing rack.** Each carpet hangs from its own brass arm, and every arm is hinged on one pole beside the carpets. The front carpet faces the viewer. Waiting carpets fan out behind it, and carpets already turned lie on the far side of the pole, showing their knotted back. "Next" and "previous" turn one page, and the order wraps round. Pressing a waiting carpet turns the rack to it. Only the front carpet can be grabbed, ideally by its lower corner, to feel its weight and sheen. Choosing another carpet resets the "pile feel" chips to that carpet's own material.
- **Hanging carpets use gravity, not a pull back to flat.** With `setHanging(true)`, the top row follows the rod (`setAnchor(angle, pivotX)`) and gravity acts on every particle. A tether keeps each particle within cloth length of its column's rod point, so the carpet never stretches like rubber. The floating studio carpet (quick view) keeps its restoring pull.
- **Material presets were retuned so silk no longer ripples like liquid.** Silk drapes and swings longest, about five seconds. Wool settles in about four seconds, and machine-made carpet in about two and a half. Wind is down to a slight breath for all three. On the rack, sheen is capped at 0.6 instead of 0.35, so silk visibly flashes against wool as it moves.
- **The arms move like an unhurried hand.** They use a critically damped spring capped at 1.7 rad/s. The solver has no air resistance, so a faster turn would throw the free edge up like a flag. Only the front carpet and carpets whose arm is still moving (plus four seconds for the swing to die out) are simulated, which keeps five carpets affordable.
- **Without WebGL** the hero shows the front carpet's photo, and the buttons still turn through the carpets.
- **Carpets never pass through each other.** Each carpet may only swing within a wedge about the pole, reaching halfway to its neighbours' arms, less a cloth's thickness. Neighbouring wedges never overlap. The arms are chained in angle order (`RackArm.chain`), and the wedge follows them as they turn.
- **The front carpet stands in the middle of the frame.** The pole sits to its right. The whole rack slides sideways as the carpets change width, and the turned pile fades out at the frame's right edge instead of being cut off.
